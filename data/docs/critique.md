# Why we rebuilt this

The first version of our team hub was tidy and mostly beside the point. Here is what was wrong with it, and what we changed.

**1. It had no goal.** It said who does what, but never why or how much. There was no 1,000, no scorecard and no definition of a good week.
*Now:* the [Road to 1,000](#/library/road-to-1000) and weekly [Numbers](#/numbers). Michelle owns the member numbers.

**2. Leah wasn't in it.** The scarcest thing in the business is Leah's voice and camera time, and everything waits on it.
*Now:* Leah has one weekly Recording Block, and everyone else's week is built around it. See the [Weekly rhythm](#/library/weekly-rhythm).

**3. It was an assembly line for one masterclass a month.** No podcast, no community, no daily touchpoint. A monthly masterclass and seven posts a week won't reach 1,000.
*Now:* the [podcast](#/library/podcast) is the front door, the free [Rep of the Day](#/library/rep-of-the-day) group is the daily habit, and the masterclass is the monthly peak.

**4. Nothing happened after someone joined.** No onboarding, no first week, no plan to keep members. If members don't build a daily rep in the first 7 days, annual members won't renew.
*Now:* Michelle owns Member Experience: onboarding, community and the 30-day check.

**5. The titles were job descriptions, and they made people smaller.** "Oversight" produces nothing. "Makes the videos pretty" describes an assistant, not the person who owns the brand's look.
*Now:* real titles, each tied to an outcome and a number, with an invitation to step up. See [Team](#/team).

**6. It created approval queues.** Michelle approved every post and Abra approved launches. Approval queues are how lean teams slow to a crawl.
*Now:* Clint publishes routine posts on his own, within the brand guidelines. Only offers, pricing and promotions need sign-off.

**7. Michelle was overloaded, and no one noticed.** The masterclass, Logan, training Clint, the calendar, every approval and status reports.
*Now:* approvals are gone. Michelle owns the member experience from start to finish, and Abra, as Brand and Mission Director and chief of staff, carries the work into the world.

**8. The tools didn't fit the team.** Tracking work in a developer tool that nobody opened every day was wishful thinking.
*Now:* this command center. One simple site where everyone sees their tasks and updates them in a tap, with a gentle reminder when something slips.

**9. Made-up numbers were presented as policy.** Days, percentages and platforms were stated as rules without asking what was realistic.
*Still true:* every number here is a **starting assumption**. We replace each one with real data within two weeks.

**10. It ignored money.** No pricing, no promotion economics, no merch budget, no cost per member.
*Now:* [Gift the Practice](#/library/gift-the-practice) includes the hat math, and Numbers tracks paid and annual sign-ups.

**11. It was public.** A public page showing internal roles and plans is a mistake.
*Now:* the site is meant to sit behind Vercel Deployment Protection, so only the team can open it, with an optional team passcode for edits.

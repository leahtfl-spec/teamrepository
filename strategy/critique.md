# Honest Critique of Version 1

Version 1 of this hub was tidy and mostly beside the point. Here's what was wrong with it, and what changed.

### 1. It had no goal
It said who does what but never why, or how much. There was no "1,000 members", no scorecard and no definition of a good week. Operations without a number is just busywork.
**Fixed:** [Road to 1,000](road-to-1000.md) and a weekly [Scorecard](scorecard.csv). Abra owns the number.

### 2. The founder wasn't in it
The scarcest resource in the business is the founder's voice and camera time, and it didn't appear anywhere. Everything downstream waits on the founder, so the plan has to be built around protecting and batching that time.
**Fixed:** The founder is now a role with one weekly recording block. Everyone else's week is built around it.

### 3. It was an assembly line for one masterclass a month
It had no podcast, no community and no daily touchpoint. A monthly masterclass and seven Instagram posts a week won't get you to 1,000 members.
**Fixed:** [Becoming You podcast](../playbooks/podcast.md) as the front door, a free [Rep of the Day community](../playbooks/community-rep-of-the-day.md) as the daily habit, and the masterclass as the monthly peak.

### 4. Nothing happened after someone joined
There was no onboarding, no first week and no plan for keeping members. It's called *the practice*: if members don't get a daily rep into their routine in the first 7 days, annual members won't renew.
**Fixed:** Michelle now owns Member Experience: onboarding, community and the 30-day check.

### 5. The titles were job descriptions, and they made people smaller
"Oversight" isn't a role, because it produces nothing. "Makes the videos pretty" describes a production assistant, not the person who owns the brand's look.
**Fixed:** Real titles, each tied to an outcome and a number, plus an explicit invitation to step up. See [Roles](../team/roles.md).

### 6. It created approval queues
Michelle approved every post and Abra approved launches. Approval queues are how lean teams slow to a crawl.
**Fixed:** Clint publishes routine posts on his own, within brand guidelines. Only offers, pricing and promotions need sign-off.

### 7. Michelle was overloaded and no one noticed
She had the masterclass, Logan, training Clint, the calendar, every approval and status reports. Version 1 gave her more work and no relief.
**Fixed:** Approvals are gone, and growth work moved to Abra. Michelle's job is now delivery and retention.

### 8. GitHub Issues for a non-technical team is wishful thinking
Be honest: will Michelle, Clint and Abra open GitHub every day? If not, the task templates are decoration.
**Recommendation:** Use this site to *read* the plan and the scorecard sheet to *track* numbers. Run tasks in whatever tool the team already opens daily. Keep GitHub only if people actually use it.

### 9. Made-up numbers were presented as policy
Version 1 stated "Day +3", "30% clips" and "Instagram/TikTok" as policy, but nobody had asked what platforms you're on or what's realistic.
**Still true:** Every number in this version is a **starting assumption**, marked as such. Replace them with real data within 2 weeks.

### 10. It ignored money
There was no pricing, no promotion economics, no merch budget and no cost per member.
**Fixed:** [Gift the Practice](../playbooks/gift-the-practice.md) includes the hat math. The scorecard tracks paid and annual signups.

### 11. The site is public
A public Vercel URL showing internal roles and processes is a mistake.
**Action:** Turn on password or login protection under Vercel → Settings → Deployment Protection.
